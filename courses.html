Short Courses for Engineering Teams

Delivered on-site, typically to groups of eight to twenty engineers. Each course assumes a working engineer's background and builds the geometry from there. No prior exposure to differential geometry is required.

LG

Applied Lie Groups for Engineers

3 daysOn-site or remoteNo differential geometry assumed

The geometry that rotations and rigid-body motions actually live on, built from an engineer's background rather than from a manifolds course.

For: engineers working on attitude determination, rigid-body dynamics, robot kinematics, pose estimation, and spacecraft or vehicle control.

Prerequisites: linear algebra, ordinary differential equations, and undergraduate mechanics.

Day 1. Rotations and rigid motions

Why Euler angles fail, and what gimbal lock is a symptom of. Quaternions and the double cover. SO(3) as a manifold, the exponential and logarithm maps, and the adjoint representation.

Day 2. Kinematics and dynamics on SE(3)

Twists, wrenches, and the Lie algebra se(3). Body and spatial frames, and the errors that come from confusing them. Rigid-body equations of motion on the group, momentum maps, and what symmetry buys you.

Day 3. Computation

Integrating on the group without drifting off it, and why re-orthonormalizing a rotation matrix is a symptom rather than a fix. Lie group integrators, interpolation and splines on SO(3), and optimization over rotations.

EST

Estimation on SO(3) and SE(3)

2 daysOn-site or remoteFollows the Lie groups course

Why filters that are well-behaved in simulation degrade in flight, and what to do about it.

For: navigation, GNC, robotics perception, and sensor fusion teams.

Prerequisites: Kalman filtering at a working level, and the material in the Lie groups course or equivalent familiarity with SO(3).

Day 1. What goes wrong

What the covariance of a rotation actually means. Why a naive EKF on quaternions is inconsistent, and how that inconsistency shows up in flight but not on the bench. Error-state versus full-state formulations, and the multiplicative EKF.

Day 2. What to do about it

The invariant EKF and the convergence properties that motivate it. Unscented filtering on manifolds. Pose-graph optimization on SE(3). Consistency testing, and how to tell when a filter is reporting a covariance it has not earned.

ADJ

Adjoint Methods and Sensitivity Analysis

2 daysOn-site or remoteDesign optimization and differentiable simulation

Where gradients come from in a simulation-in-the-loop pipeline, when they stop being the gradients of what you are actually minimizing, and how to tell.

For: teams doing shape or design optimization, PDE-constrained control, or training models through a simulator.

Prerequisites: numerical methods for PDEs or nonlinear optimization at a working level.

Day 1. Where gradients come from

Forward versus adjoint sensitivity, and when each is the cheaper choice. Continuous versus discrete adjoints. Why discretize-then-optimize and optimize-then-discretize disagree, what that costs an optimizer, and when it can be removed. Checkpointing and the memory budget.

Day 2. When gradients fail

Adjoint divergence for chaotic and long-horizon problems. Sensitivity to mesh quality, and the stabilization tricks that trade accuracy for convergence. Structure-preserving discretizations that bound long-horizon drift. How to test whether a gradient is correct rather than merely plausible.
